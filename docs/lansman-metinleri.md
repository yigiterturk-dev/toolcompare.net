## 1. Product Hunt

**Ad:** ToolCompare
**Tagline (60 karakter siniri):**
> Software tools compared on prices we actually verified

**Aciklama (ilk yorum olarak da kullanilir):**

> Hi — I built ToolCompare because every "best tools" site I landed on had
> the same two problems: a 9.4/10 score nobody could explain, and a price
> that was wrong by the time I read it.
>
> So this one does the opposite:
>
> - **No invented scores.** No stars, no 9.4/10, no "editor's rating".
>   There is nothing to fake because we never made the number up.
> - **Prices carry their source.** 243 of the 512 tools show a price we
>   pulled from the vendor's own pricing page, with the URL and the date we
>   checked it. The rest are labelled honestly: usage-based, free tier, or
>   not verified yet.
> - **Comparison pages only where there is something to compare.** If two
>   tools cost the same and do the same things, we do not publish a page
>   pretending otherwise.
>
> 512 tools, 460 with a written review, 14 categories.
>
> What I would genuinely like feedback on: is the "verified / usage-based /
> unverified" price labelling clear enough at a glance, or does it need to
> be louder? That distinction is the whole product.
>
> https://toolcompare.net

## 2. Reddit

Reddit'te link birakmak reklam sayilir; degeri ONCE metnin icinde ver,
linki sona koy. Uygun altdizinler: r/SaaS, r/Entrepreneur,
r/smallbusiness, r/webdev (arac secimi baglaminda).

**Baslik:**
> I checked the pricing pages of 512 SaaS tools by hand. Here is what I
> found about how they hide the real starting price.

**Govde:**

> I have been building a comparison site and the part that surprised me was
> not the tools — it was how hard the pricing is to read.
>
> Out of 512 tools I went through:
>
> - Fewer than half publish a flat monthly starting price you can compare to
>   anything. For the rest, "from $0" means a free tier with a seat limit, or
>   usage-based billing where the real number depends on you.
> - "Starting at" very often means annual billing, shown as a monthly figure.
>   The month-to-month price is 15-25% higher and lives one click away.
> - A surprising number of pricing pages have no date, no changelog, and no
>   way to tell whether the number changed last week.
>
> The conclusion I came to: a "best tools" ranking built on scores is mostly
> decoration. The only number on these pages you can actually check is the
> price — and even that needs a source and a date next to it.
>
> So on my own site I stopped publishing quality scores entirely. Tools are
> ordered by verified starting price, anything not comparable is labelled as
> such and sorted separately, and every price links to the vendor page and
> the date it was checked.
>
> Happy to share the site if useful, but the finding stands on its own:
> **if a comparison page shows you a score but not a source, the score is
> the product and you are not the customer.**

*(Link yalnizca istenirse, yorumda: https://toolcompare.net)*

## 4. Hacker News (Show HN)

HN uydurma pazarlama dilini cezalandirir; en kisa ve en durust hali.

**Baslik:**
> Show HN: ToolCompare – SaaS comparison with no invented quality scores

**Metin:**

> I got tired of comparison sites where a 9.4/10 appears with no explanation,
> so I built one that publishes no scores at all.
>
> What it does publish: starting prices with the vendor URL and the date they
> were checked (243 of 512 tools so far), and an honest label for the rest —
> usage-based, free tier, or not verified yet. Tools are ordered by verified
> price; anything not comparable is sorted separately and the page says why.
>
> Two things I would do differently if starting over: I began with scraped
> "ease of use" and "features" scores, and they turned out to be useless —
> every tool landed between 80 and 100, so they could not separate anything,
> yet they were driving the entire site structure. Ripping them out was the
> single biggest improvement. The second: I generated 2,345 comparison pages
> before checking whether Google would index them. It indexed almost none.
> Now a comparison page only exists in the sitemap if both sides have a
> verified price and there is an actual difference to write about.
>
> https://toolcompare.net
